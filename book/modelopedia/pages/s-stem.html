A 100 Hz feature sequence is too long for attention: 30 seconds is 3000 frames, 9 million attention scores per head. Every recognizer's encoder therefore starts with a stem of strided convolutions that trades time resolution for width, the speech counterpart of a vision model's patch embedding.

Four stems

  • Whisper: two 1-d convolutions of 3 taps, the second with stride 2, GELU after each: 50 Hz, one frame per 20 ms timestamp step{{cite:radford2022}}.
  • Voxtral Realtime: the same two convolutions, but padded on the left only, so no output frame sees the future. Its decoder then stacks four of them per step.
  • Qwen3-ASR: three 2-d convolutions, each stride 2 in time and frequency, over 480 channels, then a linear map of the flattened 480 × 16 features. It runs on 1-second chunks separately (100 → 50 → 25 → 13 frames), so its encoder runs at 13 frames a second.
  • Parakeet: FastConformer's "dw-striding" stem: a full 3×3 stride-2 conv to 256 channels, then two depthwise-separable stride-2 stages, then a linear map to the width: 8× in all, 80 ms frames{{cite:rekesh2023}}. Subsampling 8× instead of the Conformer's 4× is a large part of FastConformer's speed-up: it halves the sequence, so attention's cost falls by 4×.

Frame stacking

A projector can also subsample: Voxtral concatenates four consecutive 20 ms encoder frames and maps them through an MLP, so each 80 ms of audio becomes one decoder position. The encoder works at 50 Hz, where acoustic detail lives; the decoder at 12.5 Hz, roughly the rate of text tokens in speech.
