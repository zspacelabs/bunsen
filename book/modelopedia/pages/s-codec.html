A synthesizer's language model can't write a waveform: 24 000 samples a second is too many, and each sample carries too little meaning. It writes the codes of a neural codec instead: an autoencoder whose latent, a few dozen frames a second, is quantized into discrete tokens{{cite:zeghidour2021,defossez2022}}. bunsen loads only the codecs' decoders. The encoders that produced the training codes aren't needed to speak.

Residual vector quantization

A single codebook big enough to quantize a latent finely would need billions of entries. Residual VQ uses K small codebooks in sequence: the first picks the nearest code, the second quantizes what remains, and so on, and decoding sums the chosen vectors. K codebooks of size N give NK combinations for K·N stored vectors. The codes are ordered by importance: dropping the last few lowers the bitrate gracefully.

Mimi, Moshi's codec, split its quantizer: the first codebook is trained to carry semantic content (distilled from a speech model), the rest acoustic detail{{cite:defossez2024}}. Both codecs here follow that split. Qwen3-TTS's has 16 codebooks of 2048 at 12.5 Hz, one semantic and fifteen acoustic, decoded by two projections whose outputs are added. ZONOS3's has 10 codebooks of 4096 at 15 Hz, each code an 8-dimensional vector with its own projection, and the semantic codebook first passes through four ConvNeXt blocks.

The decoders

Both codec decoders start the same way: a trunk of causal, sliding-window transformer layers at the frame rate, with a layer scale on each sublayer, which gives every frame context from the last few seconds. Then they raise the rate:

  • Qwen3-TTS doubles twice with transposed convolutions, each followed by a causal ConvNeXt block{{cite:liu2022convnext}}, then hands the 50 Hz latent to a DAC-style vocoder.
  • ZONOS3 interleaves its trunk with patch-ups: five attention stages, each pair joined by a parameter-free reshape that halves the width and doubles the rate. The windows shrink in time as the rate grows (10 s at 15 Hz down to 2 s at 240 Hz), so every stage attends over a similar number of frames.

Two ways to the waveform

DAC's decoder (Qwen3-TTS){{cite:kumar2023}} upsamples in the time domain: four blocks of a transposed convolution (×8, ×5, ×4, ×3) and three dilated residual units, halving the width each time from 1536 to 96 channels, then one convolution to a single sample stream. Its activations are Snake, x + sin²(αx)/β, with learned α and β per channel{{cite:ziyin2020}}: a monotone function with a learned periodic ripple, which helps a network produce periodic signals, as BigVGAN showed for vocoders{{cite:lee2023bigvgan}}.

ISTFT heads (ZONOS3) don't generate samples at all. After eight ConvNeXt blocks at 240 Hz, each frame predicts a log-magnitude and a phase for every frequency bin, and an inverse STFT with overlap-add turns them into audio, as Vocos does{{cite:siuzdak2023}}. ZONOS3 runs two such heads, with FFT sizes of 736 and 2208 (fine time resolution and fine frequency resolution), mixes them with a 127-tap filter, and resamples from 44 160 Hz to 44 100 Hz. Producing a spectrum per frame is far cheaper than upsampling through convolutions, because the inverse FFT does the last ×184 for free.

Streaming a codec

Every layer before the waveform stage is causal: left-padded convolutions, causal attention over a window, and transposed convolutions that carry their overlap. A codec stream therefore carries a state per layer (each conv's last inputs, each attention stage's windowed cache), and joined chunks equal the whole decode. ZONOS3's ISTFT, mixer and resampler look a little ahead, so its stream lags by a fixed number of samples, flushed at the end. Qwen3-TTS's release renders in 300-frame chunks with 25 frames of left context, shorter than its decoder's reach; bunsen reproduces that render exactly as well as the exact stream.
