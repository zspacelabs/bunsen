All of the stride in a residual net comes from three places: the stem, each stage's first unit, and (in the shortcut) a projection or pool. This chapter covers the first two and the low-pass filters that can be added to them. Output stride, where later strides become dilation, is covered in conv blocks.

Stems

The stem takes the image from 3 channels to the first stage's width and reduces its side by 4, using one strided conv stack and one pool. It is where most of the network's spatial resolution is given up, and where conv cost per output channel is highest.

The pool has a few numerical variants, each needed to match a set of trained weights. BiT zero-pads before an unpadded max pool{{cite:kolesnikov2020}}, so on a negative pre-activation map the zeros can win. A SAME-padded max pool pads with −∞ on the bottom-right, so the pad never wins. Pre-activation nets leave the last stem conv bare, because the first unit's pre-norm normalizes it.

Anti-aliased downsampling

The fix comes from signal processing: low-pass, then subsample. An anti-aliased net moves each stride off its conv (or max pool) onto a fixed blur that runs after the activation:

convs → norm → act  ⟶  conv1 → norm → act → BlurPools

timm's variants are blur (a 3×3 binomial) and avg (a 2×2 average pool of the stride, a cruder box low-pass). In the stem, the max pool becomes a stride-1 max followed by a blur of stride 2, or a 2×2 average pool. The price is compute: a striding unit's mixer now runs at its input's resolution, about 4× the FLOPs of its strided form. The blur itself is cheap.
