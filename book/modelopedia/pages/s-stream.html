Speech arrives as a stream and is often wanted as one. A model built for whole utterances can still be streamed by running it on windows and committing what is settled. A model that is causal throughout can stream natively. The kits do both, and the difference shows up socket by socket.

Voxtral Realtime is the one recognizer whose every socket is causal, so one decoder step can run per 80 ms of audio, at a fixed delay. The others see a window at a time.

Windows and commits

bunsen's ASR driver runs any recognizer over a stream. It buffers features, decides when to decode, and places what it emits on the stream's clock. An engine that times its words (Parakeet by frames, Qwen3-ASR by alignment heads, Voxtral by position) commits the words before the widest pause in a full window's last third, and decodes the rest again with the next audio. One that can't time words cuts each window at its quietest stretch before decoding. Emission policies choose between offline (decode whole windows), conservative (decode at the end of speech) and drafting (emit provisional text and revise it); the real-time ones use a voice-activity model, Silero VAD, to find the ends of speech. Whisper's decoder also conditions on the previous window's text, which carries context across windows.

State

As in the language kits, the model never owns a stream's state. A recognizer's stream carries the front end's carry (window overlap, preemphasis), any encoder and decoder caches, and the prediction network's state; a synthesizer's carries the decoder caches and each codec layer's state. One loaded model can serve many streams at once, and a stream can be restarted by its owner.
