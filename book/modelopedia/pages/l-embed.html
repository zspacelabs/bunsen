A language model begins and ends with the vocabulary. The embedding maps each token id to a vector; the head maps the final vector back to a score for every token. Both are one matrix of shape [V, D], and in half the kits it is literally the same matrix.

The token table

An embedding is a lookup, x₀ = E[id]: equivalent to multiplying a one-hot vector by E, but costing nothing. Its row count is the vocabulary (50 368 for ModernBERT, 65 536 for nanochat d20, 151 936 for Qwen3, 248 320 for Qwen3.5, 256 000 for Gemma 1–2, 262 144 for Gemma 3), which makes it one of the largest single tensors in a small model: Gemma 3 270M spends 168M of its parameters on it. Three optional steps follow the lookup, one per family:

  • A parameter-free RMS norm (nanochat): x₀ = E[id] / rms(E[id]). The stream starts at unit scale whatever the table's rows have grown to, and nanochat 2026 feeds that normalized x₀ back into every layer.
  • A LayerNorm without a bias (ModernBERT). Its first layer then skips its own input norm, which would repeat this one (sublayers).
  • A √D scale (Gemma). Gemma's table is shared with the head. A common reading is that the head wants rows of a modest norm, and scaling on the way in lets the same rows start the stream at a scale comparable to the sublayers' writes. The scale is rounded to the table's dtype before it is applied, as upstream rounds it: in bf16, √1152 becomes 34.0.

Qwen3 and Qwen3.5 use the plain lookup, as Llama does.

The head

The head computes logits = h · Wᵀ over the final, normalized state h, then applies up to three edits, each a config field of LmHeadConfig:

  • Slice. nanochat 2026 pads its vocabulary to a multiple of 64 for the sake of the matrix shapes; the head keeps only the first V logits, so the padding rows can never be sampled.
  • Soft-cap. c · tanh(logits / c), with c = 15 in nanochat and 30 in Gemma 2. It bounds how confident the model can be, and how far one logit can run away in training (logit control).
  • dtype. The logits leave in f32 whatever the model computes in, as transformers generates from them. Sampling from bf16 logits would quantize the tail of the distribution.

Tied and untied

Tying the head to the embedding (W = E) was proposed by Press and Wolf, who showed that the output embedding is the better-trained of the two, and that sharing it improves perplexity while halving the vocabulary's parameter cost{{cite:press2017}}. Geometrically, tying says that a token is predicted when the final state points toward that token's own input vector.

The kits split by size, for a budget reason. In a small model the table is a large share of the parameters, and tying saves it: Gemma (every size), Qwen3 up to 4B, and Qwen3.5 up to 4B are tied. Larger models can afford a head of their own, which frees input and output geometry to differ: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3.5-9B and -27B, and nanochat, whose training recipe gives the head its own optimizer settings.
