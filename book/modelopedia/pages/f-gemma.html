Google's Gemma models are the open relatives of Gemini. Their three generations change more sockets per generation than any other family here, and each change answers a specific problem: an expensive cache, unstable logits, a long context. One bunsen config, GemmaConfig, covers all three. Its fields are the generations' choices.

Gemma

Design change. Against the Llama template: a GeGLU feed-forward (tanh GELU) with a hidden layer 8D wide; the embedding multiplied by √D; the head tied to it, over a 256K vocabulary; RMS norms stored as 1 + w; heads 256 wide. The 2B uses multi-query attention, and the 7B full multi-head{{cite:gemma2024}}.

Why. A 256K vocabulary makes the table a large share of a small model, which argues for tying it. The √D scale lets the shared table serve both ends (embedding). MQA at 2B cut the cache for on-device use. The very wide GeGLU and the wide heads come from Gemini's recipe. The report does not justify them separately.

Circuit key: the top row is the chassis; the board below is one layer, drawn as its two residual sublayers, with the token mixer opened up in the middle panel. Solid chips are injected components (click one for its chapter); dashed sockets are empty. Δ marks the sockets a variant changes relative to its parent.

Gemma 2

Design change. Three additions{{cite:gemma2024v2}}:

  • The sandwich: an RMS norm on each sublayer's output as well as its input.
  • Sliding-window attention (4096) on alternate layers, with full attention between.
  • Soft-caps: 50 on the attention scores, 30 on the final logits. And GQA (2:1) at every size, with a query_pre_attn_scalar setting the score scale.

Why. Gemma 2 was trained much longer and, for the small sizes, by distillation from a larger teacher. Both make stability more valuable. The sandwich and the caps each bound one place where values grow. Alternating windows cut the cost of attention at its 8K context while keeping every other layer global.

Gemma 3

Design change. QK-norm, which replaces both soft-caps. Five sliding layers to each full one, with a smaller window (512 at 270M and 1B, 1024 at 4B). Two rotary tables: θ = 10⁴ for the sliding layers and 10⁶ for the full ones, the global one scaled linearly by 8 at 4B to reach 128K tokens{{cite:gemma2025v3}}.

Why. The target was a 128K context at a modest memory cost. The KV cache of global layers dominates long contexts, so Gemma 3 made them rare and the windows short. The report shows this barely moves perplexity. Long range then lives in one layer in six, which gets its own high-θ, scaled table, while the local layers keep a fine-grained one. QK-norm gave the stability that the caps had provided, without capping the scores' range, and the caps went.
