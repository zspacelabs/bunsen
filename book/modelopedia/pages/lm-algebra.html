Every language kit in bunsen ends in the same module, LmDecoderModel: a token embedding, a DecoderStack, and a head. ModernBERT is the same stack without the head, and with its mask turned bidirectional. This chapter describes that shape, the stream that runs through it, the board of sockets each family fills, and the lowering that gets a family's config onto the board.

One model shape

The shape has not changed since GPT{{cite:radford2018}}: the Transformer's decoder{{cite:vaswani2017}} without its cross-attention, trained to predict the next token. Three things are fixed across every kit:

  • The model is isotropic. Every layer reads and writes vectors of the same width D, at every position, so the stack is a sequence of maps from ℝT×D to itself. There is no pyramid and no pooling.
  • Only the token mixer moves information between positions. The feed-forward, the norms and the head act on each position alone. Everything a model knows about order and context comes through its mixers: attention, or in Qwen3.5 also the gated DeltaNet.
  • The mask fixes the direction of time. A causal mask lets every position predict its successor in one parallel pass. A bidirectional mask turns the same stack into an encoder.

The residual stream

The residual connection{{cite:he2016}} is the organizing fact of the whole design. Since every sublayer adds to the stream rather than replacing it, the final state is the embedding plus a sum of writes, and any sublayer can read what any earlier one wrote. Elhage et al. made this the basis of reading transformers as circuits: the stream is a shared communication channel, and each head and MLP is a reader and writer on it{{cite:elhage2021}}. Two consequences recur throughout the book:

  • Norms sit on the reads, not on the stream. In the pre-norm layout every family here uses, a sublayer normalizes its own copy of the stream. The stream itself is never normalized until the final norm, so it grows as writes accumulate. That growth is why sandwich norms, logit controls and nanochat's stream lambdas exist.
  • Extra paths are cheap to add. A new input to the stream is just another addition: nanochat 2026 feeds the embedding to every layer, and adds a value table inside attention, without changing the layer's shape.

The Llama template

bunsen has no Llama kit, but the decoder that LLaMA{{cite:touvron2023}} standardized in early 2023 is the reference point most of the kits differ from. It combined PaLM's choices{{cite:chowdhery2022}} into one recipe: RMSNorm before each sublayer, a SwiGLU feed-forward, rotary positions, no biases anywhere, and (from Llama 2{{cite:touvron2023b}}) grouped-query attention. Lowering

Each kit's config (GemmaConfig, Qwen3Config, Qwen3_5Config, NanoChatGptContractConfig, ModernBertContractConfig) is a policy: the fields its upstream config has, with its defaults. Lowering turns it into a stack in one of two shapes:

  • Uniform: one layer config repeated L times (Qwen3, Gemma 1, Laya's head).
  • Patterned: a few layer configs, the stack's kinds, and a list giving each layer's kind. Each kind also names its rotary table. Gemma 2 and 3 have a sliding and a full kind, Qwen3.5 a linear and a full one, and ModernBERT three (its first layer, global, local).
  • nanochat lowers straight to the per-layer structure, because in its 2026 form the windows and value embeddings vary layer by layer.

Both unroll to the same structure, one block config per layer, which builds the DecoderStack. So "Gemma 3" is not a class with its own forward pass. It is a function from six numbers to a list of 26 block configs. Gemma, Qwen3 and Qwen3.5 hold an LmDecoderModel and nothing else, and get their forward, cache and decode from it. ModernBERT holds an embedding and a stack. nanochat is the exception: its extra paths run around the stack, so it keeps its own forward and calls the stack's with those paths as side inputs.

Laws of the algebra

  • Mixing is factored. Token mixing (across positions) and channel mixing (within a position) alternate, and never happen in the same sublayer. A family's novelty is almost always in one of the two.
  • Positions live in the mixer. No kit here adds a position vector to the stream. Rotary embeddings act inside attention's scores; the DeltaNet's conv and recurrence see order directly. The stream itself carries no position.
  • Masks are config. Causal or bidirectional, windowed or not: the same stack is a decoder, an encoder, or a local encoder, depending on one field per layer.
  • State is injected. No model owns its KV cache or recurrent state. The caller creates it and passes it to each step. See decoding state.
  • Upstream's numerics are part of the design. Where a checkpoint was trained with a particular rounding (bf16 rotary tables, the √D scale rounded to the table's dtype, an f32 softmax), the kit reproduces it, because a different rounding is a slightly different model.
