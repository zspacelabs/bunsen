Whisper{{cite:radford2022}} is the encoder–decoder transformer applied to speech, trained on 680 000 hours of weakly labelled web audio. Its architecture is deliberately the original Transformer's; its contribution was data and a multitask token format that lets one decoder transcribe, translate, identify the language and time its output. In bunsen it is the one recognizer whose decoder is not a language kit: its layers have a cross-attention sublayer.

Whisper

Design. 80 log₁₀ mel bands, floored 8 below the clip's maximum. A stem of two convolutions to 50 Hz. A pre-norm LayerNorm encoder over a fixed 30 s window (1500 frames) with a learned position table, bidirectional, GELU feed-forwards, and biases on every projection except the keys. A decoder of the same width and depth, with learned positions over 448 tokens, self-attention, cross-attention over the encoder's output, and a head tied to its embedding.

Why. The design is conservative on purpose: Radford et al. wanted to show that scale and weak supervision, not architecture, were what made robust recognition{{cite:radford2022}}. The fixed 30 s window keeps training simple; the decoder's token format does the rest:

  • a prompt of <|startoftranscript|>, a language token, a task token (transcribe or translate), and optionally <|notimestamps|>;
  • 1501 timestamp tokens, <|0.00|> to <|30.00|> in 20 ms steps, which the model writes around each segment, constrained by rules that keep them paired and increasing;
  • the previous window's text after <|startofprev|>, as context;
  • a fallback that re-decodes a window at higher temperatures when the output is too repetitive (compression ratio above 2.4) or too unlikely.

Sizes and versions

Sizes run from tiny (4 + 4 layers, width 384) to large (32 + 32, width 1280), with heads 64 wide. The .en models use GPT-2's English vocabulary and take no language or task token. Three versions change the circuit slightly:

  • large-v3 moved to 128 mel bands and added Cantonese, a 51 866-token vocabulary.
  • large-v3-turbo keeps v3's 32-layer encoder and prunes the decoder to 4 layers, then fine-tunes. Decoding dominates Whisper's latency, so this makes it several times faster for a small loss. It doesn't translate.
  • large-v1 and v2 share one geometry; v2 was trained longer.
