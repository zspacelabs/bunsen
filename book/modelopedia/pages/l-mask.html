A mask decides which keys each query may see. It is the attention's only notion of direction, and, with a window, its only hard notion of distance. In bunsen the mask is a field of the attention config, so the same stack builds a decoder, an encoder, or a windowed encoder, and a pattern of windowed and full layers is just a list of layer kinds.

Kinds of mask

  • Causal. Each query sees itself and the past. Every decoder layer.
  • Sliding (causal). Each query sees the last w positions. Gemma 2's and 3's sliding layers; nanochat 2026's short layers.
  • Bidirectional. Every query sees every key. ModernBERT's global layers and Laya's head layers.
  • Two-sided window. Each query sees ±r positions around it. ModernBERT's local layers, with r = 64.
  • Padding. An encoder runs a batch of different lengths at once, and no query may attend to a padding key. That is a mask over keys, combined with the layer's own.

Decoders and encoders

BERT showed that a bidirectional stack, trained to fill in masked-out tokens, gives far better representations for understanding tasks than a left-to-right one, because every token's state is conditioned on its whole context{{cite:devlin2019}}. The price is that it cannot generate: no step is "the next one". In bunsen the difference is one field, AttentionMask::Causal or ::Bidirectional, on the same CausalDecoderBlock. An encoder takes no cache, since it reads its input once.

Sliding windows

Full attention costs O(T²) in compute and O(T) in cache per layer. A sliding window of w costs O(Tw) and caps the cache at w. Like stacked convolutions, stacked windows still let information travel further than one window, one hop per layer. The idea came from sparse and long-document transformers{{cite:child2019,beltagy2020}}, and Mistral made it standard in decoders{{cite:jiang2023}}. A window is one-sided in a decoder and two-sided in an encoder: bunsen counts a causal window of w as the query and the w − 1 before it, and ModernBERT's ±64 as a window of 65.

Layer patterns

Windows alone lose long-range retrieval, so every windowed kit interleaves them with full layers: a full layer every few windowed ones resets the reach to the whole context, and the windows make the rest cheap. The ratio has grown over time. Gemma 2 alternated 1:1; Gemma 3 moved to 5 sliding to 1 full, with a smaller window, and found little loss in perplexity for a large cut in KV cache{{cite:gemma2025v3}}. ModernBERT puts a global layer every third{{cite:warner2024}}. Qwen3.5 applies the same logic with a different cheap layer: three gated DeltaNet layers to each full attention layer. nanochat 2026 takes its pattern as a string of S and L (upstream's default SSSL), with the last layer always long.

In the code, a pattern is a PatternedStackConfig: a few layer kinds, which may differ in window, rotary table, mixer or attention norm, and a list giving each layer's kind. On a circuit, the LAYER PATTERN panel draws that list.
