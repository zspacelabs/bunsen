A transducer is a decoder that walks the audio frame by frame. It doesn't attend over the audio; it decides, at each frame, whether to emit a token or move on. Parakeet uses the token-and-duration transducer (TDT), the latest step in a line that runs from CTC through RNN-T.

From CTC to TDT

  • CTC{{cite:graves2006}} adds a blank symbol and emits one symbol (often blank) per frame; a transcript is any path that collapses to it. Outputs at different frames are independent given the audio.
  • RNN-T{{cite:graves2012}} adds a prediction network over the tokens emitted so far, and a joint that combines it with the current frame. Emitting a token stays on the frame; emitting blank moves to the next. Outputs now depend on each other.
  • TDT{{cite:xu2023tdt}} has the joint also predict a duration: how many frames the current output covers. A decode then jumps ahead by that many frames instead of stepping through blanks one at a time.

The prediction net and the joint

Parakeet's prediction network is an embedding and two LSTM layers{{cite:hochreiter1997}} of width 640: a small language model of the transcript, started from blank. Its state is the caller's, like a KV cache. The joint is Linear(ReLU(We et + Wp gu)), whose 8198 outputs are 8193 token logits (8192 SentencePiece pieces and blank) and 5 duration logits for 0 to 4 frames.

Greedy TDT decoding

  1. At frame t, score the joint on frame t and the prediction network's current output.
  2. Take the best token and, independently, the best duration.
  3. If the token isn't blank, emit it with its frame, and step the prediction network on it.
  4. Advance t by the duration. A blank with duration 0 is forced to 1, and so is any frame that has already emitted 10 tokens, so the decode always ends.

Every emitted token carries its frame and duration, which give word timestamps on the 80 ms grid without any alignment step. Because most frames of speech carry no new token, skipping them makes TDT decoding much faster than RNN-T's{{cite:xu2023tdt}}.
