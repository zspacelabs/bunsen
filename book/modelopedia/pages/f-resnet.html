ResNet{{cite:he2016}} is the root of the lineage and the most heavily refined member. The original idea, stages of residual units with identity shortcuts, has stayed fixed. Over six years nearly every slot around it has been revised. This chapter covers the design changes that define its sub-families. bunsen's ResNet kit covers all of them, along with ResNeXt and Wide ResNet.

ResNet v1

Design. A 7×7 stride-2 stem with a 3×3 max pool. Four stages at stride 1, 2, 2, 2. Units are basic (two 3×3s) for the shallow nets and bottleneck (1×1 → 3×3 → 1×1, expansion 4) for the deep ones. A 1×1 projection on each stage's first unit, BN and ReLU everywhere, and global average pooling into one linear layer.

Slot fill. unit = basic | bottleneck · mixer = 3×3 conv · shortcut = 1×1 conv · stem = 7×7 + max · no attention, no anti-aliasing, no final layer.

Depth is a list, not a design change. Depth (18 through 152 and beyond) only changes the unit count per stage, and the basic/bottleneck switch follows depth. Neither creates a new family, so the kit treats them as one design. The widely used "stride on the 3×3" form (torchvision's, called ResNet-B) moves the stride from the first 1×1 to the mixer, so the path no longer discards ¾ of its input{{cite:he2019tricks}}.

The "bag of tricks" stems and shortcuts (C, D, S, T, E)

He et al. 2019 collected cheap fixes to the two places where ResNet v1 throws information away{{cite:he2019tricks}}:

  • C: deep stem. The 7×7 becomes three 3×3s (widths 32, 32, 64): the same reach, more nonlinearity, slightly more compute.
  • D: average-down shortcut. The projection shortcut's stride moves onto a 2×2 average pool before the 1×1 (why). D also uses the deep stem.
  • timm adds S (a deep stem at width 64), T (a tiered deep stem with widths 24, 32, 64, plus avg-down) and E (a 64-wide deep stem plus avg-down){{cite:timm}}.

Slot fill. stem = deep | tiered · shortcut = avg → 1×1. The units are unchanged.

Channel attention: SE-ResNet and ECA-ResNet

Squeeze-and-excitation{{cite:hu2018}} gates each unit's residual branch with channel gains computed from the branch's global average. ECA{{cite:wang2020eca}} replaces SE's bottleneck MLP with a 1-d conv across channels. Both fill the attention slot at the output placement and leave every other slot as it was, so each attention net is "its base ResNet (or ResNeXt) plus a gate": SE-ResNeXt, ECA-ResNet-D, ECA-ResNeXt-T, and so on. The "light" ECA variant only changes the depth list.

Anti-aliased ResNets

Each stride-2 unit keeps its mixer at stride 1 and hands the stride to a low-pass pool after the mixer's activation. The stem's max pool becomes either a max at stride 1 followed by a blur of stride 2 (blur nets) or a 2×2 average pool (aa nets){{cite:zhang2019aa}}. The result is more shift-invariant, and usually more accurate, at the cost of running each striding mixer at its input's resolution. It combines freely with D and SE (SE-ResNeXt-AA-D).

ResNet-RS

Bello et al. 2021 asked how much of the progress since ResNet came from architecture and how much from training and scaling{{cite:bello2021}}. Their architecture changes are modest: an SE gate at a reduction of ¼, the deep stem with its max pool replaced by a strided 3×3 conv, and avg-down shortcuts. The scaling rule is the real result: increase depth when overfitting is not a concern, and increase resolution slowly. That rule only affects the depth list, so in the algebra ResNet-RS is "ResNet-D + SE¼ + conv stem pool".

A related point from the same period: ResNet-50 trained with a modern recipe ("ResNet strikes back") reaches 80.4% top-1 with no change to the architecture{{cite:wightman2021}}. Weight sets differ by recipe, not by design, so this book does not separate them.

Norm variants

Replacing every BN with a 32-group GN (stem included){{cite:wu2018gn}} is a pure layer-choice change: the norm is stated once and reaches every conv block. It is the same knob BiT uses, there combined with pre-activation and weight standardization.

Descendants

Every later conv family starts from a ResNet policy and changes the mixer, the norm placement or the width rule. ResNeXt groups the mixer. ResNetV2 moves the norms. Res2Net, SKNet and ResNeSt replace the mixer while keeping the rest of a ResNet-D. RegNet replaces the hand-set widths with a generated rule.
