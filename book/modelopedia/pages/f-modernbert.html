ModernBERT applies six years of decoder improvements to the BERT encoder{{cite:warner2024}}. In the algebra it is the plainest demonstration that an encoder is a decoder stack with a different mask: bunsen builds it from the same DecoderStack and CausalDecoderBlock as Qwen3, with the mask set to bidirectional.

From BERT

BERT is a post-norm Transformer encoder with learned absolute positions, GELU MLPs, biases everywhere, and a 512-token context, trained to fill in masked tokens{{cite:devlin2019}}. It and its descendants dominated retrieval and classification for years after decoders took over generation, because a bidirectional state is the better representation of a whole input.

ModernBERT

Design change. Against the Llama template, which it largely adopts: a bidirectional mask; LayerNorm without a bias instead of RMSNorm; an exact-GELU GeGLU; a LayerNorm on the embedding, with the first layer's attention norm dropped; and a pattern of local and global layers, a global layer every third, the others attending ±64 tokens, each kind with its own rotary table (10⁴ local, 1.6·10⁵ global). No projection has a bias.

Why. The aim was an encoder with an 8K context that runs fast on common GPUs. Local attention makes two layers in three cheap at long lengths, and global layers every third keep whole-document context. RoPE, GeGLU and pre-norm are the decoder lessons it adopted. The bias-free LayerNorms follow the same bias-free choice as the projections. ModernBERT also unpads its batches during training, which is an execution detail, not part of the circuit.

mmBERT

mmBERT is ModernBERT's architecture trained on over 1800 languages, with Gemma 2's 256K-token vocabulary{{cite:marone2025}}. Its base model has the same 22 × 768 geometry. On the board only the EMBED socket changes (its row count), so it gets no circuit of its own. bunsen meets it as the encoder of laya-multilingual.

No head

The kit's model ends at the final norm: token ids in, one normalized hidden state per token out, padding masked by each item's length. A model built on it (a classifier, a retriever, a decision head) holds the encoder and loads it from its own checkpoint's prefix. The next chapter's Laya is one.
