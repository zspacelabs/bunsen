Qwen3-ASR is a recognizer built by giving a chat language model ears: a Whisper-style audio encoder whose frames take the place of placeholder tokens in a Qwen3 prompt{{cite:qwen2025asr}}. Everything after the splice is the Qwen3 kit, unchanged.

Qwen3-ASR

Design change. Against Whisper:

  • The front end is Whisper's with 128 bands, levelled per 30 s window.
  • The stem is three 2-d strided convolutions over each 1-second chunk, 100 frames to 13, then a linear map.
  • The encoder adds sinusoidal positions that restart every chunk, and attends within 8 s blocks. Every projection has a bias.
  • The bridge is a splice: a two-layer GELU MLP maps each frame to the text width, and the frames replace the <|audio_pad|> tokens of a chat prompt (system, user with the audio, assistant).
  • The decoder is Qwen3 (0.6B or 1.7B), and replies language English<asr_text>…, naming the language it heard before the transcript.

Why. Splicing reuses everything a chat model already has: its tokenizer, its training, its context, and its decoding. The encoder only has to produce vectors the decoder can read as if they were tokens. Chunked positions and block attention bound the encoder's cost on long recordings, and 13 frames a second keeps the audio's share of the context small.

Circuit key: four rows, audio in to audio out, each read left to right. Solid chips are components (click one for its chapter); dashed sockets are empty. Δ marks the sockets a variant changes relative to its parent.

Word timing. The model writes no timestamps. bunsen recovers them from eight of the decoder's attention heads that track the audio: their attention from each reply token onto the audio positions is averaged and aligned monotonically (a dynamic-time-warping path), and each token is placed at its peak.
