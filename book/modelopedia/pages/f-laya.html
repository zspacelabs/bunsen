Laya is an open decision model{{cite:laya2025}}. It is given a state and a typed question (pick one of several named options, choose a level on a scale, or answer yes or no) and returns calibrated probabilities over the options, in one forward pass, without generating text. In the algebra it is an encoder and a small task head: the ModernBERT (or mmBERT) circuit, then two more encoder layers and a scorer.

The model

Design. The prompt lays out the question, each option preceded by a [MASK] marker, then the state, cut to the release's token budgets (512 or 1024). The encoder reads it bidirectionally. A learnt embedding of the question's kind (choice, score or yes/no) is added at every position. Two head layers mix the result over the prompt's tokens. Then the head scores each marker.

Why. Scoring markers turns a multiple-choice decision into a per-position readout, which a bidirectional encoder does well, and it works for any number of options. Putting the options in the prompt lets each option's state attend to the state and to the other options, so the scores are comparative. The kind embedding tells the shared head which of three output conventions it is answering in.

The head

The two head layers are PyTorch's stock TransformerEncoderLayer as upstream builds it: pre-norm, biased LayerNorms and projections, max(1, d/64) heads with no rotary positions, and a ReLU MLP of 4D. Positions reach the head only through the encoder's states. In bunsen they are a two-layer DecoderStack with a bidirectional mask and no final norm.

The scorer is a LayerNorm and an MLP (d → d, exact GELU, d → 1), applied at each marker, and positions past the question's options get a logit of −10⁴. An act head reads the first token's state with four statistics of the answer distribution (its top probability, its margin, its normalized entropy, and the option count) and scores a fixed set of costed actions and "none".

Calibration

A softmax's probabilities are rarely calibrated: a model that says 0.9 is right less than 90% of the time. Temperature scaling, dividing the logits by a T fitted on held-out data, fixes most of that{{cite:guo2017}}. Laya fits one temperature per question kind and per bucket of option count (2, 3–5, 6–10, 11+) and ships them in its release's config. The answer is softmax(logits / T), computed in f64. That calibration is part of the model's contract, not of the network.

The three releases share this design: the English model on ModernBERT-large (28 × 1024), the multilingual one on mmBERT-base (22 × 768, a 256K vocabulary), and a typed-decision model trained on typed questions with 1024-token prompts.
