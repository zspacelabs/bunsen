A normalization layer standardizes activations over some set of axes, then applies a learned per-channel affine map γ x̂ + β. The kinds differ only in which axes the statistics are taken over, and that one choice decides whether the layer depends on the batch.

Batch norm

x̂n,c,h,w = (x − μc) / √(σ²c + ε),  μc, σ²c over (N, H, W)

BN is the default norm of the lineage{{cite:ioffe2015}}. At inference its statistics are frozen running averages, so BN becomes an affine map per channel and folds into the preceding conv. In the residual unit, BN's γ on the last norm of the path controls the branch's initial magnitude. Initializing it to zero makes every unit start as the identity{{cite:goyal2017}}, which is the geometric reading of residual learning.

Group norm

GN splits channels into 32 groups and normalizes each group over its channels and space, per sample {{cite:wu2018gn}}. It does not depend on the batch, which matters for transfer at small batch sizes, so BiT uses it throughout. Layer norm{{cite:ba2016ln}} is the one-group case. GN on its own trains worse than BN on ImageNet. Paired with weight standardization, it closes the gap.

Weight standardization

EvoNorm-S0

EvoNorm-S0 was found by searching over computation graphs that fuse a normalization and an activation into one layer{{cite:liu2020evo}}:

y = x·σ(v·x) / stdgroup(x) · γ + β

The numerator is a SiLU-like gate with a learned slope. The denominator is a group-norm-style scale without mean subtraction. It takes the place of a "norm + act" pair and does not depend on the batch. In the slot algebra it is a norm whose activation is its own, so the layer choice for activation is ignored where it is used (ResNetV2-EvoNorm).
