A transformer block never changes the shape of its stream, so a staged transformer has to downsample between blocks. Swin's patch merge does it: each 2×2 neighbourhood of tokens becomes one token with four times the channels, which is then reduced to twice the width{{cite:liu2021swin}}. The side halves and the width doubles, as at the first unit of a ResNet stage.

The merge

Rearranging a 2×2 block into channels and then applying a 1×1 projection is the same function as a 2×2 conv with stride 2. It is the inverse of the sub-pixel "pixel shuffle"{{cite:shi2016pixelshuffle}} followed by a pointwise map. So the merge is, once again, a strided conv: the operation behind the stem and the patch embedding, now used between stages.

Stages

A staged encoder opens stages 2 to 4 with a merge. Stage i is C·2i wide, and its head count grows with it, so the head width stays 32. The stage grids at 224 pixels with 4-pixel patches are 56, 28, 14 and 7, the strides of a ResNet's stages. The windows stay M×M tokens throughout, so each window covers four times the image area at each stage, much as a conv's receptive field grows with stride.

Where a ResNet's reshaping unit needs a projection shortcut, the merge sits outside every residual. The merge itself has no skip connection, and every block's shortcut stays the identity. That is the same choice RegNetZ makes when it gives reshaping units no residual at all, here applied to the whole stage boundary.

Used by Swin V1, Swin-S3, Swin V2; it sits inside the STACK chip of their circuits
