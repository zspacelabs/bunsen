Since 2021 the strongest ViTs have mostly been trained as the image towers of multimodal systems: matched against captions, made to predict text and pixels, or plugged into language models. Their training signals differ a great deal, but what they change in the encoder is small, and the circuits make that plain. Every model in this chapter is a prefab of the same ViT policy. Classification heads are timm's additions on top of the towers.

CLIP

Design change. A LayerNorm on the token sequence before the first block (ln_pre), a patch embedding with no bias, LayerNorm ε of 10⁻⁵, and in OpenAI's original weights the QuickGELU activation{{cite:radford2021clip}}.

Why. CLIP trains an image tower and a text tower contrastively on hundreds of millions of image–caption pairs, so that matching pairs land close together in a shared space. That makes a classifier from nothing but class names ("zero-shot"), and features that transfer unusually well. The extra norm normalizes the sum of patch and position embeddings before the residual stream starts, so the patch projection needs no bias. Later CLIP-style weights (LAION, DataComp, MetaCLIP) keep the tower and change the data. The _quickgelu names mark weights trained with OpenAI's activation.

SigLIP

Design change. No class token. The readout is a multi-head attention pool ("map") over the patch tokens{{cite:zhai2022scaling}}, and some sizes use the tanh approximation of GELU.

Why. SigLIP replaces CLIP's batch-wide softmax with a per-pair sigmoid loss, which trains well at any batch size{{cite:zhai2023siglip}}. The loss doesn't touch the encoder. The tower keeps Google's big_vision conventions: no class token, and a MAP head. Its so400m shape (27 × 1152, MLP ratio 3.74) was fitted by scaling laws for compute-optimal shapes{{cite:alabdulmohsin2023}}.

{{circuit:siglip|The class token goes; a learnt query's attention pool reads the patches.}}

The _gap towers are the same weights with the attention pool removed and the patch tokens averaged. This is how PaliGemma reads the SigLIP tower, at up to 896 pixels{{cite:beyer2024paligemma}}.

{{circuit:siglip-gap|The attention pool becomes a mean over the normalized patch tokens.}}

EVA

EVA{{cite:fang2023eva}} pretrains a plain ViT by masked image modelling, with CLIP features as the reconstruction targets. timm's eva_large geometries in the ViT kit are mean-pooled ViT-Ls with the norm after the pool. On the board that is the ViT circuit with POOL = avg, the FINAL NORM moved to POOL NORM, and nothing else.

BEiT3

Design change. Sub-LN: an extra LayerNorm inside each branch, on the attention's output before WO and on the MLP's hidden width before fc2. Mean pool, no class token.

Why. BEiT3 is a "multiway" transformer, pretrained by masked modelling on images, text and image–text pairs together{{cite:wang2022beit3}}. Its blocks follow Magneto's sub-LN, an initialization-and-norm scheme from Foundation Transformers that keeps very deep stacks stable across modalities{{cite:wang2022magneto}}. The vision-only classifier keeps the image expert's path.

{{circuit:beit3|The norm placement stays pre-norm; the new norms live inside the token and channel mixers.}}

AIMv2

Design change. A language model's block: RMSNorm everywhere (on the patch tokens too), a SwiGLU MLP of ratio about 2.7, SiLU, and no biases on any projection. No class token, mean pool, norm before the pool.

Why. AIMv2 pretrains the image encoder with a multimodal decoder that autoregressively generates the image's patches and then its caption{{cite:fini2024aimv2}}. Building the encoder from the decoder's parts (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, no biases) is the generative-pretraining convention, and those parts work as well in an encoder.
