Each layer is two residual sublayers, and each sublayer has norm sockets around its branch. bunsen's ResidualSublayer has four (input, pre, post, out), plus a layer scale and stochastic depth; the language kits use two of them. This chapter covers the kind of norm, how its gain is stored, and where it goes.

LayerNorm and RMSNorm

LayerNorm subtracts each vector's mean and divides by its standard deviation{{cite:ba2016}}. Geometrically, it projects onto the hyperplane orthogonal to the all-ones vector, then onto a sphere. RMSNorm skips the centring and divides by the root mean square{{cite:zhang2019rms}}. That is cheaper, and Zhang and Sennrich found that re-centring contributes little: scale invariance is what stabilizes training. Every decoder kit uses RMSNorm, at ε = 10⁻⁶. ModernBERT and Laya use LayerNorm: ModernBERT's without a bias (in line with its bias-free projections), Laya's head with one.

How the gain is stored

A norm's gain γ multiplies its output channel by channel. Gemma and Qwen3.5 store it as w and apply 1 + w, so a freshly initialized (or weight-decayed) gain of zero means "pass through" rather than "silence". bunsen loads such a checkpoint's w as 1 + w and applies the gain as stored, so the forward is one kind of norm for every kit. nanochat's norms have no gain at all; bunsen's norms always have one, and loads it as ones, which is the same function. Qwen3.5's DeltaNet has one more norm, a gated RMSNorm, whose gain is stored as applied (DeltaNet).

Placement

The Transformer normalized after the residual add, norm(x + f(x)). That post-norm layout needs learning-rate warm-up and grows unstable with depth, because the gradient to early layers passes through every norm. Moving the norm onto the branch's input, x + f(norm(x)), leaves a clean identity path from output to input, which is why GPT-2 and every decoder since use it{{cite:radford2019,xiong2020}}. Pre-norm is the transformer's version of ResNetV2's pre-activation. Every language kit is pre-norm, with two variations:

  • The sandwich (Gemma 2 and 3): a second norm on the branch's output, before the add: x + norm₂(f(norm₁(x))). Pre-norm leaves the size of each write free, and the stream grows as writes accumulate. The output norm fixes every write's scale. It costs two norms per layer, and Gemma's are 1 + w, so each sublayer's contribution starts at unit scale.
  • A skipped input norm (ModernBERT): the first layer's attention reads the stream as it is, since the embedding's LayerNorm has just normalized it.

The final norm

Because the stream is never normalized in a pre-norm stack, the head reads it through one last norm, after the final layer. Every kit has one: RMSNorm in the decoders, LayerNorm in ModernBERT. Laya's head stack has none, and its scorer normalizes each marker's state itself.
