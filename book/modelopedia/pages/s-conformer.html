The Conformer{{cite:gulati2020}} is the speech encoder that combined attention with convolution: attention for global, content-based mixing, a depthwise convolution for local, offset-based mixing. Parakeet's FastConformer encoder{{cite:rekesh2023}} is a Conformer with a faster stem. In bunsen it is a third kind of layer for the same encoder stack, built from the same residual sublayers.

The macaron block

A Conformer block has four pre-norm residual sublayers where a transformer layer has two. The feed-forward is split into two halves, each added at half weight, placed before and after the mixers: the "macaron" shape, which Gulati et al. took from Lu et al.'s reading of a transformer layer as one step of an ODE solver. A final LayerNorm closes every block. Feed-forwards use Swish (SiLU) at 4× width; Parakeet's have no biases.

The convolution module

The depthwise convolution gives every channel its own short filter over time (9 frames, 720 ms, in Parakeet). That is what attention lacks: a cheap, translation-invariant detector of local patterns such as formant transitions, whatever their absolute position. The GLU in front makes it input-gated, like the gated MLPs. It is the one place in the book where BatchNorm appears in a transformer layer.

Relative positions

The Conformer's attention uses Transformer-XL's relative positional encoding{{cite:dai2019}}. A score is the sum of a content term and a position term: (qi + u) · kj + (qi + v) · WP p(i − j), where p is a sinusoidal table over the 2T − 1 possible offsets, and u, v are learned per head. The position term is computed against all offsets at once and then shifted into place (the "rel-shift"). One table serves every layer. Compared with RoPE, which rotates the content vectors so that position appears inside q·k, this keeps position as a separate additive term with its own projection, at the cost of a T × 2T product per head.
