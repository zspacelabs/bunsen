Attention by itself is blind to order: permute the keys and values together and every output is unchanged. Every decoder kit here tells attention where tokens are with the rotary position embedding (RoPE){{cite:su2021}}, which has no parameters, adds nothing to the stream, and makes every score a function of relative position.

Positions as rotations

RoPE splits each head of width d into d/2 pairs, treats each pair as a point in a plane, and rotates it by an angle proportional to the token's position. Because rotations preserve dot products up to their difference,

⟨R(mω) q, R(nω) k⟩ = ⟨q, R((n − m)ω) k⟩,

the score between two tokens depends on their content and their offset, never on their absolute positions. Since it acts on queries and keys, not the stream, it applies to the attention layers only, and a key cached at step n keeps its rotation for good. Positions are not stored anywhere else in the model.

The base and the spectrum

The frequencies ωᵢ = θ−2i/d form a geometric series from 1 radian per token down to about 1/θ. Fast pairs resolve fine order nearby; slow pairs separate distant positions. A pair whose wavelength is longer than any context seen in training never completes a turn, so its angle always stays in a range the model has learned. Long-context models raise θ to put more pairs in that regime, which is known as adjusting the base frequency{{cite:xiong2023}}. The kits trace that trend:

Two tables

A stack can hold more than one rotary table and give each kind of layer its own. Gemma 3 does: its sliding layers only ever see 512 or 1024 tokens back, so they keep θ = 10⁴ and a fine-grained spectrum, while its full layers use θ = 10⁶ for long range{{cite:gemma2025v3}}. ModernBERT does the same with 10⁴ for its local layers and 1.6·10⁵ for its global ones{{cite:warner2024}}. On the board this is the POSITIONS socket reading "two RoPE tables", and the stack's pattern says which layers take which.

Partial rotary

Qwen3.5's attention layers rotate only the first quarter of each head. The rotated span still holds a full spectrum, since the frequencies are spread over it alone. The remaining 192 dimensions score pure content similarity, at any distance. In a hybrid stack this is reasonable: the DeltaNet layers around each attention layer already encode order through their convolution and recurrence, so the attention layer can spend most of its width on retrieval by content.

Scaled tables

A model can be extended past its training length by dividing every frequency by a factor f, so that position p is rotated as p/f. Every angle then stays within the trained range, at the cost of resolution between neighbours. A short fine-tune recovers most of it{{cite:chen2023pi}}. Gemma 3 4B applies this to its global table, with f = 8, to reach 128K tokens; its local table is left alone.

Conventions

Two choices don't change what RoPE computes, but a checkpoint is trained under one of them, and applying the other silently ruins it:

  • Which dimensions pair up. transformers' rotate_half pairs dimension i with i + d/2 (Llama, Qwen, Gemma, ModernBERT, nanochat). RoFormer's original pairs adjacent dimensions. Converting between them is a permutation of WQ's and WK's rows.
  • Which way it turns. nanochat rotates with the opposite sign to transformers. Scores depend only on the angle between query and key, so a model is fine either way, but only the way it was trained.

nanochat's 2025 model also kept its rotary tables rounded to bf16, and its checkpoints were trained with those rounded angles. The kit reproduces the rounding; the 2026 model computes exact tables.
