nanochat is karpathy's minimal, end-to-end ChatGPT clone: tokenizer, pretraining, mid-training, fine-tuning, RL and serving, in one small codebase meant to be read{{cite:karpathy2025}}. Its model is a GPT with each part replaced by its modern counterpart, and it is the one kit here that bunsen also trains from scratch (examples/train-chat). Upstream has published two versions of the model, and bunsen loads checkpoints of both.

From GPT-2

GPT-2 fixed the decoder-only shape: learned absolute positions, LayerNorm moved before each sublayer, a final LayerNorm, a GELU MLP of 4D, biases everywhere, and a head tied to the embedding{{cite:radford2019}}. nanochat keeps the shape and replaces nearly every part: rotary positions instead of learned ones, parameter-free RMS norms instead of LayerNorms, no biases, ReLU² instead of GELU, QK-norm, and an untied, soft-capped head.

nanochat (2025)

Design. A uniform stack whose geometry is one number, the depth d: width 64d, heads 128 wide (so d/2 of them), as many key/value heads as query heads, a 2048-token context. The embedding is normalized by a parameter-free RMS norm, every norm is parameter-free, QK-norm follows RoPE (θ = 10⁴, tables rounded to bf16), the MLP is 4D ReLU², and the untied head's logits are soft-capped at 15.

Why. nanochat optimizes for a model that trains well on a small budget (the d20 run cost about $100), so each choice is the simplest one known to be stable. Parameter-free norms are fewer parameters to train and tune. QK-norm and the logit cap bound the two places where logits can blow up at high learning rates. ReLU² is Primer's cheap win{{cite:so2021}}. An untied head lets the input and output geometries differ, and lets the head train under its own optimizer settings.

nanochat (2026)

Design change. Four extra paths around the stack: the smear, per-layer λ and μ that rescale the stream and mix the embedding back in, value embeddings on alternate layers, and the mid-stack backout. Three changes to attention: a QK gain of 1.2, sliding windows from a pattern (SSSL by default: short windows of a quarter of the context, the last layer always long), and θ = 10⁵ with exact tables. The vocabulary is padded to a multiple of 64, and the head slices it back.

Why. Each change bought a measurable drop in the loss reached on a fixed budget. Most are additions to the stream, which cost almost nothing per token. The value embeddings add parameters, but only as lookup tables. The windows cut attention's cost in three layers of four. In bunsen these are config options, all off by default and switched on together by with_upstream_2026. Loading a checkpoint identifies its version from its tensor names and meta file, and refuses a mixture of the two.
