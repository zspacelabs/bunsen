Both families answer the same question: how to make the bottleneck's mixer wider. ResNeXt splits it into many narrow groups{{cite:xie2017}}. Wide ResNet simply makes it wider{{cite:zagoruyko2016}}. In the algebra both are a single ResNet knob, the mixer width ⌊planes · base_width / 64⌋ · cardinality, applied to a grouped or ungrouped mixer.

ResNeXt: cardinality

The claim. At a fixed FLOP and parameter budget, raising cardinality (more, narrower groups) improves accuracy more than raising depth or width{{cite:xie2017}}. A grouped mixer spends its budget on more channels, each mixing spatially within its group, and the dense 1×1s on either side mix across groups.

Slot fill. A ResNet with mixer groups = cardinality and mixer width from base_width. The 32x4d in a name is 32 groups of 4 channels per 64 planes. Everything else is inherited, so ResNeXt takes the same tricks as ResNet (D stems and shortcuts, SE, ECA, anti-aliasing) without any new structure.

Wide ResNet

Zagoruyko and Komodakis argued that depth was over-emphasized: a much shallower but wider residual net matches a very deep thin one and trains faster{{cite:zagoruyko2016}}. The ImageNet versions in the kit (wide ResNet-50-2 and 101-2) keep ResNet's depths and double only the bottleneck's mixer width (base_width 128): the same knob as ResNeXt, with one group.

Descendants

Cardinality is the axis three later families build on. SKResNeXt and ResNeSt make the grouped mixer attentive. RegNet keeps the grouped bottleneck as its only unit and searches over its widths and group sizes.
