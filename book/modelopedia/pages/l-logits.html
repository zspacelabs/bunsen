A softmax is only as good as the range of its inputs. When attention scores grow too large, the softmax saturates to one-hot, gradients vanish, and large models' training can diverge. Each kit uses some combination of four controls on the scores and the final logits. Which ones a family uses is one of the clearest markers of its generation.

Why scores grow

A score q·k = |q||k| cos α grows with the norms of the queries and keys, and nothing in plain attention bounds those. Dehghani et al. found that at 22B parameters, attention logits grew without bound early in training and the softmax collapsed to near one-hot{{cite:dehghani2023}}. Wortsman et al. reproduced the same instability in small models trained at high learning rates, and showed which fixes remove it{{cite:wortsman2023}}.

QK-norm

QK-norm normalizes each head's query and key before the dot product{{cite:henry2020}}, so |q·k| is bounded by the norms' gains. With RMS norms, it is the fix both papers above adopted. Qwen3 introduced it to the Qwen line while dropping Qwen2's QKV biases{{cite:yang2025qwen3}}. Gemma 3 adopted it in place of Gemma 2's soft-cap{{cite:gemma2025v3}}. nanochat applies it with no learned gain at all, so every query and key is a vector of the same length, and the score is a scaled cosine similarity.

Where the norm sits relative to RoPE matters only when the norm has a gain: a rotation preserves the RMS, but not a per-dimension weighting. With a learned gain, the order must match the checkpoint, and it differs between families.

The score scale

The default scale, 1/√d, keeps scores near unit variance for random unit-variance q and k{{cite:vaswani2017}}. Two kits set it explicitly:

  • Gemma 2 and 3 scale by query_pre_attn_scalar−½ = 256−½. In the 2B, 9B and every Gemma 3 model the head width is also 256, so the change does nothing there; it exists for Gemma 2 27B, whose heads are 128 wide but whose scale is taken from D/H = 144{{cite:gemma2024v2}}.
  • nanochat 2026 multiplies its normalized queries and keys by a gain g = 1.2. With the norm pinning their lengths, that is a sharper temperature, g²/√d.

Soft-capping

A soft-cap passes small values through unchanged and squashes large ones toward ±c. Bello et al. used it to keep a pointer network's logits in range{{cite:bello2016}}. Gemma 2 caps attention scores at 50 and the final logits at 30{{cite:gemma2024v2}}; nanochat caps its final logits at 15. Unlike QK-norm, a cap acts on the whole score, so it also bounds the effect of the score scale and of any bias. Gemma 3 dropped both of Gemma 2's caps when it adopted QK-norm.

The softmax in f32

The last control is numerical. Every decoder kit runs its attention softmax in f32 even when the model computes in bf16, as transformers does. With bf16's 8 bits of mantissa, a softmax over tens of thousands of keys rounds most small weights to zero, and their sum drifts away from one. Logits leave the head in f32 for the same reason.
